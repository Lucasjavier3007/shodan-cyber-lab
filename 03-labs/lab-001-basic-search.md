### Important interpretation

The presence of a vulnerability finding in Shodan should not automatically be interpreted as proof that the underlying vulnerability is currently exploitable.

Shodan distinguishes between verified and unverified vulnerability findings. Unverified vulnerabilities may be inferred from metadata such as detected software versions and require additional verification.

Therefore, the findings should initially be treated as **indicators requiring validation**, rather than confirmed exploitable vulnerabilities.

## Vulnerability Analysis Questions

For each finding, a defensive assessment should consider:

1. What CVE is associated with the finding?
2. Which product or component is affected?
3. What version is associated with the finding?
4. Is the vulnerability verified by Shodan?
5. What is the severity?
6. Is the affected service currently exposed?
7. Is the vulnerable component actually present and active?
8. Is there a patched version available?
9. Could compensating controls reduce the exposure?
10. Does the finding represent a current risk or historical exposure?
